Journey to the
Center of the Earth

Grab a seat. This trip is 6,371 km long, and your scroll wheel is the only vehicle you need.

Humans have barely dented the planet we live on: the deepest hole ever drilled is just 12 km. So let's go much further, one scroll at a time, and see what is down there.

0 – 35 km

The Crust

The thin, cool shell we live on. Under the oceans it is only about 5 km thick, and under mountains it can reach 70 km.

12 kmdeepest hole drilled

35 – 410 km

The Upper Mantle

Solid rock that flows extremely slowly, a few centimetres a year, about as fast as your fingernails grow. This drift moves entire continents.

~900 °Cat the top
~1,300 °Cfurther down
Did you know? Most natural diamonds form 150 km or more below the surface and are carried up by rare volcanic eruptions.
💎
410 – 2,890 km

The Lower Mantle

The biggest layer by volume, making up over half of the planet. Pressure here squeezes rock into dense crystal forms that do not exist at the surface.

1,900 – 3,700 °Ctemperature
~1.3 milliontimes air pressure
Did you know? Scientists think huge blobs of denser material sit at the base of the mantle under Africa and the Pacific. Nobody knows exactly what they are.

2,890 – 5,150 km

The Outer Core

Liquid iron and nickel, churning like a giant pot. These moving metals act like a dynamo and create Earth's magnetic field, which protects us from solar wind and makes compasses work.

4,000 – 5,000 °Ctemperature
2,260 kmthick
Did you know? Earth's magnetic north wanders and has flipped completely many times in history, roughly every few hundred thousand years.
🧲
5,150 – 6,371 km

The Inner Core

A solid ball of iron and nickel, roughly the size of the Moon. It is hotter than a Sun-like surface but stays solid because the pressure is so intense.

~5,400 °Ctemperature
~3.6 milliontimes air pressure
1,220 kmradius
Did you know? The inner core is slowly growing, by about a millimetre a year, as the liquid outside freezes onto it.

Let's put it in perspective

How Big Is This Trip?

6,371 km sounds abstract, so here is what it looks like in everyday terms.

  • By car at 100 km/h: about 64 hours of non-stop driving, nearly three days.
  • By plane at 900 km/h: a little over 7 hours, shorter than a flight from India to Europe.
  • On foot at 5 km/h: around 53 days of walking without sleep.
  • Falling through a tunnel: in theory about 42 minutes, if there were no air and no heat.
Times are simple estimates, rounded for fun.
Common mix-ups

Myths vs. Reality

Myth: The Earth's center is a giant ocean of lava.
Reality: The mantle is solid rock that flows very slowly. Only small pockets near the surface melt into magma.
Myth: The core is the hottest place we know.
Reality: It is about as hot as the Sun's surface, but the Sun's center is thousands of times hotter.
Myth: We could just drill down and look.
Reality: Heat and pressure would destroy any drill long before it got close. Even 12 km was a huge struggle.
Why it matters

Why Should We Care?

What happens down there shapes life up here. The magnetic field from the outer core shields us from harmful space radiation. Moving mantle rock builds mountains and causes earthquakes and volcanoes. And heat flowing out of the Earth powers geothermal energy, which countries like Iceland already use to heat homes.

A little thought to take with you

Still Mostly a Mystery

Here's what I find most amazing: we know the surface of Mars and the Moon in far more detail than the ground under our own feet. Everything you just scrolled through was figured out indirectly, from waves, magnets and clever maths. There is still plenty left for the next generation of scientists to find out.

How do we know all this?

Listening to Earthquakes

Nobody can dig this deep, so scientists study seismic waves. Earthquake waves speed up, slow down, bend, or vanish depending on what they pass through. By reading them, we mapped the whole interior, and found that liquid layer in the middle.
